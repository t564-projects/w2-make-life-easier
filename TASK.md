# Week 2 Task: Make Life Easier

## Part 1: Complain-storming

The first step in building something useful is identifying what's annoying, tedious, or unnecessarily complicated in your life. We're starting with complaints!

### Complain-storming

Spend 20 minutes complaining with someone in your life about tasks that are annoying to you. This could be a friend, family member, roommate, or classmate. The goal is to generate a long list of friction points, repetitive tasks, or processes that feel unnecessarily difficult.

Make a list of these tasks in your design journal. Don't worry about whether they're "solvable" yet—just capture the complaints!

Here are some ideas:

- Scheduling meetings across time zones
- Splitting bills with roommates
- Organizing digital files
- Tracking when to water plants
- Deciding what to cook based on what's in the fridge
- Managing group project tasks
- Remembering to bring an umbrella
- Organizing reading notes

The more specific your complaints, the easier it will be to imagine solutions. Instead of "email is overwhelming," try "I spend 20 minutes every morning sorting through newsletters I never read."

Document your brainstorming session in your journal—you can include photos, voice notes, sketches, or text. The format doesn't matter; what matters is capturing the annoyances you want to address.

## Part 2: Focusing

Now that you have a list of complaints, it's time to identify 2–4 specific automation ideas that you could build this week.

### Focusing

For each idea, document the following in your design journal:

1. A short description of the annoying thing you want to make easier. What's the problem you're solving?
2. A description of the process before. What steps do you currently have to take? What makes it annoying?
3. A description of what you imagine the process would be after you make your life hack. How would this automation change the experience? What would happen automatically?

Remember, you'll be building this in class with about 1.5 hours of work time, so keep the scope manageable! It's fine to:

- Focus on one specific use case rather than a comprehensive solution
- Prioritize automation that saves time or reduces friction
- Think "functional prototype" rather than polished product

If you're having trouble coming up with ideas, here are three examples that might inspire some new thinking:

- **Word Tally**: A tool that reports information about a piece of text like word count and reading time, all calculated within the browser.
- **House Chore Dashboard**: An interactive checklist that tracks recurring household tasks, shows who completed what last, and reminds roommates when it's their turn. Uses state to remember completion history.
- **Poetry Everyday**: A script that creates a daily pop-up on your computer that displays a random poem from an online poetry database.

The clearer your vision before class, the more time you'll have to experiment and build during our session!
